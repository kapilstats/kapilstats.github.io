Statistical Inference–II

M.Sc. Statistics · Semester III · Academic Session 2026–27

CCS University, Meerut 4 credits · 60 lectures 60 lecture decks

The course gives a unified treatment of statistical decision theory, sequential procedures and distribution-free methods: how loss and risk functions provide a common framework for estimation and testing, how the sample size can be decided sequentially through an SPRT, and how valid inference can be made when no fully specified parametric model is available.

Syllabus

Unit Topics Lectures
I Decision theory: loss and risk functions; randomised and non-randomised decision rules; admissibility; complete, essentially complete and minimal complete classes; Bayes and minimax decision rules; estimation and testing viewed as decision problems; Bayes and minimax estimators; Bayes and minimax tests in simple cases. 20
II–III Sequential analysis: SPRT and its applications to binomial, Poisson, normal, exponential and gamma populations; O.C. and A.S.N. functions; termination of the SPRT with probability one; Wald’s fundamental identity and its applications. 20
IV–V Nonparametric inference: probability integral transformation; estimation of quantiles and distribution-free confidence intervals for population quantiles; tests for randomness; runs and sign tests (one and two samples); median test; Wilcoxon and Mann–Whitney tests; one- and two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests. 20
