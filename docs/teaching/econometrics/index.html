Econometrics

B.Sc. (Hons) Statistics · Semester V · Academic Session 2026–27

CCS University, Meerut 4 credits · 60 lectures 60 lecture decks

The course covers how econometric models are built and estimated, and what to do when the assumptions of the classical linear regression model fail: generalized least squares, multicollinearity and ridge regression, heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation, instrumental variables, and the identification and estimation of simultaneous equations models.

Syllabus

Unit Topics Lectures
I Introduction: objective behind building econometric models, nature of econometrics, model building and role of econometrics. Generalized least squares estimation, Aitken estimator, Gauss–Markov theorem for GLSE. 15
II Problem of multicollinearity, its implications; sources of multicollinearity; tools for handling the problem; remedies for multicollinearity; ridge regression. Heteroskedasticity, consequences and tests for it, estimation procedures under heteroskedastic disturbances, Bartlett’s test, Breusch–Pagan test and Goldfeld–Quandt test. 15
III Autocorrelation: concept, consequences of autocorrelated disturbances, detection and solution of autocorrelation. Instrumental variable estimation. 15
IV Simultaneous equations model, problem of identification, necessary and sufficient condition for the identifiability of parameters in a structural equation, ordinary least squares, indirect least squares, two-stage least squares and limited information maximum likelihood method. 15
